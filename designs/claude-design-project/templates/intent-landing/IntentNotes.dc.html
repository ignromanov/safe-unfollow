The page is a printed answer with one door in it

Drawn end to end for /instagram-pending-follow-requests: it is the page with the least room to over-promise, the one whose honest result is often empty, and the one whose demo is thinnest. A layout that survives it survives the other two; the reverse is not true.

Obligations, in my order

  1. B · the answer. The reader typed a question; the first paragraph closes it. Also where the doorway defence starts.
  2. A · the h1. Confirms the search result kept its promise. On mutuals it also does the §4 scoping — it is the one h1 that cannot wrap shorter.
  3. E · the click. One marked button, 48px, directly under the answer. Everything else is arranged so the distance answer → button is under one thumb.
  4. F · reassurance at the click. Belongs at the click and nowhere louder: the distrust is of the upload, so the sentence sits on the upload's door. Its second half already appears inside B.
  5. G · the body. Second in policy weight, fifth in fold order. Prose, no accordion — hidden text is still crawled, but the page's own reader should not have to tap to read the thing that justifies it.
  6. C + D · the proof, disclosed. One obligation, not two: D is a property of C. Four rows, count-led, disclosure at the same weight as the count.
  7. H · second CTA. Exists, unmarked, after the body. Crafted, not argued.
  8. I · sibling nav. Two links, after H.
  9. J · Header/Footer. Not an obligation of this page — a constraint on it. Drawn as inherited stand-ins.

Q2–Q10, in one line each

Q2 Yes. Four rows, count-led, directly after the CTA on mobile and beside it on desktop. Leaning: a specimen that cannot show their list does not satisfy the curiosity that brought them — so no reduction in follow-through. A lean, not a finding.

Q3 Same card. The footer sentence and the fourth h2 say "empty is normal" out loud; 30 is typeset at the same size as 150 and looks as small as it is.

Q4 Structurally: the proof footer's job (caveat / empty case / scoping), the mutuals h1 at three lines, and pending's fourth h2 being an empty-state document. Otherwise the same scaffold — the three-documents test is on text, and the text is different.

Q5 Premise corrected: HTML exports are now read, so the JSON lever is gone. What the page still does is name the wait before the reader meets it ("No export yet?" card, 2 min / a day or more, "this page will be here").

Q6 Drawn as shipped. Token fix: lower L of the chip text in light theme to ≈0.5 (pending 0.65→0.5), keep the 12% fill. Cost: BADGE_STYLES becomes two maps (light/dark), every /results row and stat tile changes tone.

Q7 No primary-as-text. Links: foreground, 600, underline in primary/50, offset 3px; hover = solid underline. Property-wide — proposed as the default a rule, not a page exception.

Q8 No dependency. If you ever want to know whether H earns anything, add a position value to the payload; the layout does not need the answer.

Q9 Different above the fold only: two columns, answer | proof card. The second column holds proof, which is not an action. Body returns to one 680px column; nothing sticky.

Q10 Token swap holds, with one check: 12% chip fills on a 0.18 L card are near-invisible, so the 20% border carries the chip. The primary/30 glow reads as a halo on dark — acceptable, not designed for.

Q11 · premises I think are wrong

  1. §10.1, the HTML-format lever. Stale: HTML exports are accepted. The brief's #1 lever is a fixed bug, not a page design problem. What remains of §10.1 is the wait, and no page copy shortens Meta's email.
  2. §8, ~45 characters a line. Measured 190 characters → 5 lines at 358px, ≈38–40 ch. The 220 ceiling is 6 lines = 156px, a fifth of the fold.
  3. §11, C and D as two items; J as an item. D is a property of C. J is a constraint, not an obligation — it has no placement decision.
  4. §10.4 vs §3. "The button's position on 390px is the number that matters" is a prior stated as a measurement, on a page where the two positions are not separable. Keep it as a prior.
  5. §2, the most consequential unknown. Not "do they know what an export is" but "do they hold one now" — it splits the audience into people who can click today and people for whom the page can only set an expectation.
  6. §11 "copy is given verbatim." The answer sentences and the eight handles are not in the brief or the project; the body prose is described by heading only. Everything here in those slots is draft and is labelled so.
